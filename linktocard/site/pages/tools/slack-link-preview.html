    What it checks

    • The og:site_name, og:title, og:description and og:image tags, their twitter: twins, the page's <title>, meta description and favicon.
    • The image the tags point to: whether it loads without a login, its type, file size, pixel size and shape.

    What it does not claim

    • We could not read Slack's own documentation about unfurling, so this page makes no Slack-specific claim: not which tag Slack reads first, not its text lengths and not its cache time. The checks are the general ones every link preview depends on.
    • It cannot see what Slack has already cached for your link.

    FAQ

    Questions about Slack unfurls.

    Short answers, and where we are not sure, we say so.

    Why does my link not unfurl in Slack?

    The causes this page can test are on your side: a missing tag, a page that needs a login, a slow answer or an image that does not load. The checklist above covers each of them. The checker does not run scripts, so tags that a script adds after the page loads are not seen; put them in the HTML your server sends.

    Where does the site name in the mockup come from?

    From og:site_name, and the page's domain when that tag is missing. Set og:site_name to the short name of your site so every app can show it.

    I fixed my tags. Why does Slack still show the old preview?

    Apps cache what they fetched for a link, and we could not confirm Slack's cache time. A URL with a small change, such as ?v=2 on the end, is usually treated as a new link and fetched fresh.

    Is this a Slack tool?

    No. It is an independent checker made by [[site.name]], not by Slack, and the unfurl it draws is a mockup.

    Is it free? What does the checker keep?

    Yes, and you do not need an account. There is an hourly limit on fresh checks from one network, and after a few checks you may be asked to confirm you are a person. The checker keeps the parsed tags and the check results for [[inspect.cacheMinutes]] minutes, never the page's HTML. Privacy policy
