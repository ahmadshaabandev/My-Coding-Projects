Choosing a chart

Which chart for which data.

The five types

  • Bar compares amounts between categories: sales per region, answers per option. Use horizontal bars when the labels are long. Bar graph maker
  • Line shows how values change over time, such as visitors per month, and compares the trends of a few series. Line graph maker
  • Area is a line chart with the area under the line filled. Stacked, it shows how parts build a total over time.
  • Pie and doughnut show the parts of one whole, with a few slices. Pie chart maker

When in doubt, use bars: lengths are easier to compare than angles or areas.

How to enter the data

  • Each row is one label: a category, a month, a slice. Each column after the label is a series, with its name at the top.
  • To bring data from a spreadsheet, copy the cells with the series names in the first row and the labels in the first column, and paste them into any cell of the table. The table takes their shape.
  • A pie or doughnut chart uses the first series only, and its values cannot be negative.
  • Switching the type keeps your data, so you can try each type on the same numbers.

The image

The PNG is drawn at twice the size you choose, so it stays sharp on high-density screens. The email size is made for the usual 600 pixel wide email: show the image at that width. Choose the dark theme for a dark slide or page; the chart itself stays plain, so it fits any design.
