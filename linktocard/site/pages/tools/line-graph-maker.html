About line charts

When a line chart works, and when it does not.

Good for

  • Change over time: visitors per month, revenue per week, a price over the years.
  • Comparing the trends of a few series on one chart, each in its own colour.
  • Many points: a line stays readable where bars would get thin. Some labels on the axis are left out when they do not fit; the points are all drawn.
  • An area chart, the line with the area under it filled, to stress the amount. Stacked, it shows how parts build a total over time.

Better as another chart

  • Categories with no order, such as regions or products: a bar chart. A line between them suggests a change that is not there.
  • The parts of one whole at one moment: a pie chart or a stacked bar.
  • Many series that cross each other: beyond a few lines, the chart is hard to read. Show fewer series, or one chart each.

How to enter the data

  • One row per point in time, oldest first, with its label in the first column: Jan, 2026-03, Week 12.
  • Labels are text. The chart draws them in the order of the table, evenly spaced, and does not read them as dates, so a missing month is not shown as a gap: add its row.
  • Every point needs a value. A row with an empty cell is refused with a message that names it.
  • One column per line. Give each a name at the top, and the legend shows it.
