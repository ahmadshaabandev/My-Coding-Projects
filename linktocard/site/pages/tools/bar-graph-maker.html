About bar charts

When a bar chart works, and when it does not.

Good for

  • Comparing amounts between categories: sales per region, sign-ups per plan, answers per option.
  • Two or three series side by side, such as this year against last year for each quarter.
  • Stacked bars, when the series are parts of a total: each bar shows the total and how it is made up.
  • Horizontal bars, when the labels are long or there are many categories: the labels then sit on the left and are easier to read.

Better as another chart

  • A trend over many periods, such as daily or monthly values: a line chart shows the change more clearly.
  • The parts of a single whole, with only a few parts: a pie chart can work, though bars compare the sizes more precisely.
  • Many series at once: beyond a few, side-by-side bars get thin. Show fewer series, or several charts.

How to enter the data

  • One row per category, with its label in the first column. Each further column is a series: give it a name at the top, and the legend shows it.
  • To compare years or groups, add a series for each with Add series, or paste a block with the names in its first row.
  • The bars follow the order of the table. Sort the rows in your spreadsheet first if you want them from largest to smallest.
  • Negative values are drawn on the other side of the zero line.
