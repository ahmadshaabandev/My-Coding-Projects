About pie charts

When a pie chart works, and when it does not.

Good for

  • The parts of one whole: how a budget is split, where orders come from, the answers to one question.
  • A few slices whose sizes differ clearly. Each slice gets its own colour, and the legend names them.
  • A doughnut chart is the same chart with a hole in the middle. It reads the same way; choose the look you prefer.

Better as another chart

  • Many slices, or slices of nearly the same size: a bar chart, because lengths are easier to compare than angles.
  • The same parts in several groups or years: a stacked bar chart, one bar per group.
  • Values over time: a line chart.
  • Numbers that are not parts of a whole, such as prices or scores: a bar chart.

How to enter the data

  • One row per slice: its label and its value. The chart works out each slice’s share from the values, so they do not have to be percentages.
  • Values must be zero or more; a negative value is refused with a message that names its row.
  • With Show values, each slice shows its value, with the prefix or suffix, such as %. When a value does not fit in its slice and the legend is on, the values are shown in the legend instead.
  • A pie uses the first series only. Extra columns stay in the table, dimmed, for the other chart types.
