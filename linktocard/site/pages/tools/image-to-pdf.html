    About the tool

    What it does, and what it does not.

    What it does

    • Puts each image on its own page, in the order of the list: A4, US Letter or A5, or a page the size of the image (one pixel is one point, 1/72 inch).
    • Turns each page to suit its image, unless you choose portrait or landscape, and centres the whole image within the margin, or fills the page and crops what does not fit.
    • Places JPEG photos in the PDF without compressing them again, so they keep their quality. PNG images are stored without loss, except that a 16-bit PNG is reduced to 8 bits per colour, which looks the same on screen and in print; WebP and GIF images (a GIF’s first frame) are turned into PNG in your browser first.
    • Shows phone photos the right way up: a photo taken sideways is turned by its orientation tag, as photo viewers do.
    • Leaves photo metadata out of the PDF: the place, time and camera a phone stores in a photo are not copied, only the picture.

    What it does not do

    • It does not read other formats, such as HEIC, TIFF or SVG. Save them as JPEG or PNG first.
    • It does not shrink images. A large photo stays large, and so does the PDF.
    • It does not read the text in a scan (no OCR): the pages are pictures.
    • PNG, WebP and GIF images may have up to [[localPdf.maxImageMegapixels]] million pixels each, because they are unpacked in memory. JPEG photos are not unpacked, so they have no pixel limit.

    Your files stay on your device

    The PDF is made in this browser tab, with the memory and processor of your computer or phone. Your images are not uploaded, so we never see them, their names or their contents, and nothing about them is counted or logged. The previews in the list come from your browser’s memory too.

    Because it all happens on your device, the limits are about memory, not money: up to [[localPdf.maxFiles]] images and [[localPdf.maxTotalMb]] MB together per PDF. The page uses the open-source library pdf-lib; its licence travels with the code, in pdf-local.js.

    Automate this with the API

    To turn images into PDFs from your own app, send their addresses to POST /v1/pdf-tools/from-images with an API key from your dashboard. The options mean the same as here; the margin is in points. The API takes PNG and JPEG images; convert WebP and GIF files first.

    curl [[site.url]]/v1/pdf-tools/from-images \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer ltc_your_api_key" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "files": [
      { "url": "https://example.com/receipt-1.jpg" },
      { "url": "https://example.com/receipt-2.png" }
    ],
    "page_size": "a4",
    "fit": "fit",
    "margin": 28,
    "response": "binary"
  }' \
  -o receipts.pdf

    Each started block of [[toolkit.pagesPerUnit]] pages of the result counts as one image of your plan. On the Free plan, every page of an API result carries a small “[[pdf.freeMark]]” line. Read the PDF toolkit docs

    FAQ

    Questions about images to PDF.

    Short answers about privacy, quality and page sizes.

    Are my photos uploaded?

    No. The page reads the images you choose in your browser and makes the PDF there. The download comes from your browser’s memory, not from our server. We never receive the images, so we cannot keep, read or share them.

    Will my photos lose quality?

    No. JPEG photos go into the PDF as they are, without being compressed again. PNG, WebP and GIF images are stored without loss (a 16-bit PNG is reduced to 8 bits per colour, which looks the same on screen and in print), which can take more room in the PDF than the original file did.

    Which page size should I choose?

    A4 or US Letter for anything that will be printed or sent as a document, such as receipts or scans. “Same as each image” for screenshots and pictures that should be shown as they are, with no white border.

    My photo is sideways. Why?

    The page turns a photo by the orientation tag the camera stored in it, the way photo viewers do. A photo that is sideways in your photo viewer too was saved that way; turn it there, save it, and add it again.

    Is there a watermark?

    No. Nothing is added to your pages, and there is no sign-up.
